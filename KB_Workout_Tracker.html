KETTLEBELL TESTS

▼

Snatch Test

100 reps in 5 min. Switch hands as needed. The classic KB benchmark.

TGU Test

Half-bodyweight TGU, 1 rep each side, clean form. The total-body strength gate.

Goblet Squat Hold

20kg held at chest, full depth, 1 minute. Pure leg + core endurance.

Farmer Carry

Hold 2× bodyweight (combined). Walk for distance. Grip + posterior chain test.

BODYWEIGHT TESTS

▼

Max Pull-Ups

Strict, dead-hang to chin-over-bar. One unbroken set.

Max Push-Ups (2 min)

As many strict reps as possible in 2 minutes. Rest allowed in plank position.

Max Dips

Parallel bar, full depth (shoulders below elbows), lock out at top.

Pistol Squat

Max consecutive reps per leg. Full depth, controlled descent.

THE KETTLEBELL BIG 5 — PHILOSOPHY

▼

This program is built on the StrongFirst / Pavel Tsatsouline approach: minimum exercises, maximum carryover. Five core movement patterns hit every muscle group, every session. No isolation work. No fluff.

The five patterns:

PatternMovementWhat it builds
Hinge / PowerSwing (2H → 1H → hand-to-hand → double)Posterior chain, conditioning, power output
SquatGoblet → Front → Offset → DoubleQuads, glutes, core stability
PressStrict → Push Press → See-saw → DoubleShoulders, triceps, vertical pushing
PullClean + Row variationsBack, biceps, posterior chain
Total BodyTurkish Get-Up (8 → 12 → 16 → 20 kg)Full body strength, mobility, stability

A 6th pattern, Carry, finishes each session and trains grip + core under load. Phases 2+ add Snatch as a power-endurance lift.

3 sessions per week. Full body every session. Workout A and B alternate.
Rest 2-3 min on strength lifts. Rest 90s on swings and carries.
Sleep 7-9 hours. Walk every day. No supplements needed except creatine (optional).
Progress by reps → density → variation → load. Add weight only when you have exhausted the first three.

COOL-DOWN ROUTINE — EVERY SESSION (7-8 MIN)

▼

This is where the rebuild starts. Skipping cool-down isn't lazy — it actively slows progress. Treat it like part of the workout.

Block 1: Decompress the spine (1-2 min)

Dead hang from pull-up bar30 sec × 2 setsResets the spine after swings, cleans, snatches

Block 2: Hip flexor + posterior chain release (2-3 min)

Couch stretch60 sec each sideSwings, squats, walking all tighten hip flexors
Pigeon or 90/90 hip stretch60 sec each sideGlute and external rotator release

Block 3: Shoulder + thoracic release (2 min)

Doorway pec stretch30 sec each sideCounters the press and push-up shoulder forward position
Thread-the-needle8 slow reps each sideThoracic mobility, keeps overhead position healthy

Block 4: Breath down (2 min)

Crocodile breathing2 min, face downSwitch out of fight-or-flight, drop HR to baseline
Post-session habit stack (2 min):
1. Log the session (sets, reps, weight, variation)
2. Rate readiness 1-10 for next session
3. Hydrate 500 ml water
4. Note any pain or breakdown in form

DELOAD WEEK — WHAT IT IS & HOW TO DO IT

▼

A deload is a planned easy week after 6 weeks of hard training. You still train, but lighter and shorter. This lets the body catch up, repair connective tissue, and reveal your actual new fitness level.

You cannot skip the deload. Skipping it is the single fastest way to plateau or get hurt in this program.

What to doWhat to keep the same
Drop to 1 lighter bell per lift (e.g. 20 → 16 swing)Train the same 3 days that week
Cut sets in half (5 sets → 2-3 sets)Keep all rest periods normal
Stop 4-5 reps before failure on every setSleep 7-9 hours, walk daily, eat normally
Skip the snatch and double-bell workWarm-up and cool-down still mandatory
A deload should feel easy and almost too short. If you finish sweating buckets, you went too hard.

PROGRESSION METHOD — REPS → DENSITY → VARIATION → LOAD

▼

You progress through these levers in order. Exhaust each before moving to the next. This is how you get 6-12 months of progress without buying more bells.

LeverWhat changesExample
1. RepsSame weight, more reps per set20 kg swing 5×10 → 5×15 → 5×20
2. SetsMore total volume5×10 → 8×10 → 10×10
3. DensityLess rest, same work10 swings EMOM 60s → EMOM 45s → EMOM 30s
4. VariationHarder version of same movement2H swing → 1H swing → hand-to-hand → double
5. LoadHeavier bell, lower reps to start16 kg press 5×8 → 20 kg press 5×3 ladder
Rule of thumb: when you hit the top of the rep range on all sets with 2-3 reps still in reserve for 2 sessions in a row, advance to the next lever.

Beginner Notes

  • Swing: hinge at the hips, not the knees. The bell floats, you don't lift it. Snap the hips through. Bell to chest height max.
  • Goblet squat: hold bell at chest, elbows tucked in. Squat between the legs, knees track over toes, full depth.
  • Strict press: rack the bell on the forearm (not the wrist). Press straight up, lock out overhead. No leg drive.
  • TGU: 7 steps each side. Master the movement with 8 kg before adding load. Watch any Pavel TGU video for cues.
  • Single-arm row: hinge forward, one hand braced on a chair, row the bell to the hip. Pull with the back, not the arm.

KEY RULES

▼
RuleWhy
Rest 2-3 min on strength lifts (press, squat, TGU).Full recovery between sets means more quality reps.
Rest 90 sec on swings and carries.Power output stays high, conditioning still builds.
Do not train to failure.Stop at 2-3 reps in reserve. Failure on KB = bad form = injury risk.
Stay in each phase 6 full weeks minimum.Adaptation takes time. Rushing means skipping connective tissue gains.
Never skip the deload week.Accumulated fatigue masks fitness. A deload reveals your true strength.
TGU is technique-first.Stay at lighter bell until form is clean. Don't grind a heavy TGU.
Snatch hand insertion matters more than weight.Drop weight before grinding bad reps. Tear-free snatches = sharp form.
Walk every day.20 min minimum. Non-negotiable.
Sleep 7-9 hours.No supplement replaces sleep.
Log every session.Track weight, reps, variation. Use the tracker tab.
